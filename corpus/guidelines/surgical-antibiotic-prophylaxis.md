# Surgical Antibiotic Prophylaxis

> Synthetic teaching document. Illustrative values only. Not for clinical use.

## Timing

Give the prophylactic antibiotic within 60 minutes before the surgical incision. Vancomycin and fluoroquinolones need longer infusions and should be started within 120 minutes before incision.

## Standard agent and dose

Cefazolin is the first-line agent for most clean and clean-contaminated procedures. Give cefazolin 2 g IV for adults under 120 kg and 3 g IV for adults weighing 120 kg or more.

## Beta-lactam allergy

For patients with a history of anaphylaxis to beta-lactams, use clindamycin 900 mg IV or vancomycin 15 mg/kg IV. Patients with a non-severe penicillin allergy can usually receive cefazolin.

## Redosing

Redose cefazolin every 4 hours during long procedures, and after blood loss greater than 1500 mL. Clindamycin is redosed every 6 hours. Vancomycin does not usually need intraoperative redosing.

## Duration

Prophylaxis should not continue beyond 24 hours after surgery for most procedures. Longer courses increase the risk of Clostridioides difficile infection and antimicrobial resistance without reducing surgical site infection.
