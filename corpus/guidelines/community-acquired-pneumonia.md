# Community-Acquired Pneumonia in Adults

> Synthetic teaching document. Illustrative values only. Not for clinical use.

## Diagnosis

Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) is suspected in a patient with new cough, fever or dyspnoea and focal chest signs. Diagnosis requires a new infiltrate on chest imaging. A procalcitonin level alone should not be used to decide whether to start antibiotics.

## Severity assessment

Use the CURB-65 score to support, not replace, clinical judgement. Award one point each for confusion, urea > 7 mmol/L, respiratory rate >= 30 breaths per minute, systolic blood pressure < 90 mmHg or diastolic <= 60 mmHg, and age 65 years or older. A score of 0 or 1 usually supports outpatient treatment. A score of 2 suggests hospital admission. A score of 3 or more indicates severe pneumonia and early assessment for intensive care.

## Outpatient treatment

For adults without comorbidities, first-line therapy is amoxicillin 1 g orally TID. Doxycycline 100 mg orally BID is an alternative, including for patients with penicillin allergy. For adults with comorbidities such as chronic lung, liver or kidney disease, use amoxicillin-clavulanate 875 mg orally BID plus a macrolide, or a respiratory fluoroquinolone as monotherapy.

## Penicillin allergy

Clarify the history before avoiding all beta-lactams: many recorded penicillin allergies are not true IgE-mediated reactions. For a documented non-severe reaction, a third-generation cephalosporin can usually be used. For a history of anaphylaxis to penicillin, avoid beta-lactams and use doxycycline 100 mg BID or a respiratory fluoroquinolone. Record the reaction type in the allergy list.

## Inpatient treatment

For non-severe inpatient CAP, use a beta-lactam such as ceftriaxone 1-2 g IV once daily plus a macrolide. For severe CAP, add coverage based on local resistance patterns and prior microbiology. Obtain blood cultures and sputum culture before the first antibiotic dose when this does not delay treatment.

## Duration and follow-up

Treat for a minimum of 5 days. Stop antibiotics once the patient has been afebrile for 48 hours and is clinically stable. Routine follow-up chest imaging is not required in patients whose symptoms resolve.
