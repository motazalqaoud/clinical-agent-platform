# Acute Kidney Injury and Renal Drug Dosing

> Synthetic teaching document. Illustrative values only. Not for clinical use.

## Definition and staging

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is defined by a rise in serum creatinine of 26.5 umol/L or more within 48 hours, a rise to 1.5 times baseline or more within 7 days, or urine output below 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 hours. Stage 1 is creatinine 1.5 to 1.9 times baseline. Stage 2 is 2.0 to 2.9 times baseline. Stage 3 is 3.0 times baseline or more, or initiation of renal replacement therapy.

## Immediate management

Assess volume status and treat hypovolaemia. Review all medications and stop or withhold nephrotoxic drugs, including NSAIDs, aminoglycosides and iodinated contrast where possible. Withhold ACE inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers during the acute episode and restart them once kidney function recovers.

## Drugs to avoid or adjust

Avoid nitrofurantoin when eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m2, because efficacy falls and toxicity rises. Reduce the dose of gabapentin when creatinine clearance is below 60 mL/min. Reduce the dose of renally cleared antibiotics according to estimated creatinine clearance and check the local formulary for adjusted doses.

## Monitoring

Measure serum creatinine and potassium at least daily while AKI is evolving. Treat potassium > 6.5 mmol/L as an emergency. Monitor fluid balance and daily weight.

## Referral

Refer to nephrology for stage 3 AKI, AKI with no clear cause, or complications not responding to medical treatment such as refractory hyperkalaemia, acidosis or fluid overload.
