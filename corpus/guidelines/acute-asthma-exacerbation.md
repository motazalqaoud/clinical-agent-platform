# Acute Asthma Exacerbation in Adults

> Synthetic teaching document. Illustrative values only. Not for clinical use.

## Severity assessment

Assess severity using peak expiratory flow (PEF), respiratory rate, heart rate, ability to speak in sentences and oxygen saturation. A PEF between 33% and 50% of best or predicted indicates an acute severe exacerbation. A PEF below 33%, SpO2 < 92%, silent chest, cyanosis, exhaustion or altered consciousness indicates life-threatening asthma.

## Oxygen

Give controlled oxygen to maintain a target SpO2 of 93% to 95%. Avoid uncontrolled high-flow oxygen once the target is reached.

## Bronchodilators

Give a short-acting beta-agonist (SABA): salbutamol 5 mg by oxygen-driven nebuliser, repeated every 15 to 30 minutes as needed. Add ipratropium 0.5 mg by nebuliser every 4 to 6 hours for severe or life-threatening exacerbations.

## Corticosteroids

Give systemic corticosteroids within one hour of presentation: prednisolone 40-50 mg orally once daily for at least 5 days. If the patient cannot take oral medication, give hydrocortisone 100 mg IV every 6 hours. Tapering is not necessary for courses shorter than 14 days.

## Escalation

Consider magnesium sulfate 2 g IV over 20 minutes for acute severe asthma that has not responded to initial bronchodilator therapy. Refer to critical care for life-threatening features, rising PaCO2 or worsening hypoxaemia despite treatment.

## Discharge

Before discharge, check inhaler technique, provide a written asthma action plan and ensure the patient has a maintenance inhaled corticosteroid. Arrange review in primary care within 2 working days.
